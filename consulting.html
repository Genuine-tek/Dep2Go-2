The Breakdown Is Not What Costs You the Week

Here is the part that catches people by surprise. Turning the wrench is usually the quick bit — a couple of hours, maybe an afternoon. The days you actually lose go to hunting the piece down, waiting on a truck, and signing for it when it finally shows up.

Which is how a forty dollar fitting ends up holding a three hundred thousand dollar excavator hostage for a week. The machine is not really broken any more. It is just waiting.

This is where thirty years earns its keep. That is how long we have spent building the network — salvage yards, rebuilders, machine shops, and suppliers still holding stock for machines the manufacturer walked away from a decade ago. It is also how long we have spent learning which brands hold up, where a non O.E.M. part will serve you just as well, and when the only answer left is to have one made.

We know what an idle machine costs, because we have spent a career on the other end of that phone call. Getting you moving again, fast, is not a side benefit of what we do — it is the entire job!

The Parts We Keep Close

Some parts cost you an afternoon. These ones cost you the week. So they stay at the front of our minds — lined up with more than one supplier each, so nobody’s backorder becomes your problem, and out the door the day you call. Here is what we watch, machine by machine.

Excavators

Hose and fitting sets in the sizes you run, hydraulic and engine filters, fuel filters, belts, bucket teeth, track pads, seal kits for the cylinders.

Why: a burst hose stops the machine where it stands, and teeth and pads wear out on their own schedule whether you are ready or not.

Wheel Loaders

A spare tire in your size, hoses, cutting edges, the full filter set, belts, pins for the articulation joint, brake pads.

Why: loader tires are thousands apiece and rarely sitting nearby, and cutting edges disappear a little more every shift.

Dozers

Track shoes, sprockets, idlers, roller seals, cutting edges and end bits, hoses, filters, ripper tips.

Why: the undercarriage eats about half of what a dozer costs you to run, and a worn sprocket against a fresh shoe chews through both.

Cranes

Wire rope in the exact spec, sheave bearings, hoses and filters, limit switch assemblies, brake pads, a set of outrigger pads.

Why: rope is a safety item with rules about when it comes off, and a bad sheave means nothing gets lifted at all.

Dump Trucks

Hoses for the tipping gear, PTO parts, brake pads and drums, air system bits, tail lights, tailgate pins and bushings.

Why: a truck that will not tip is not a truck, and brakes and air lines are what inspectors write up first.

Every Machine You Own

Hydraulic fluid and engine oil in bulk, coolant, grease, air filters, fuel and water separator elements, battery terminals, bulbs.

Why: these get used by everything in the yard. Run dry on hydraulic fluid and the whole site stops at once.
